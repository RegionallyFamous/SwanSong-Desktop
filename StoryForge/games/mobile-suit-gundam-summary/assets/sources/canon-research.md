# Canon research packet — original television continuity

## Scope

This adaptation summarizes the 43-episode Japanese television series that
began broadcasting on April 7, 1979. It does not silently import changes from
the compilation films, *The Origin*, the novels, later Universal Century
retcons, games, or modern remakes.

Primary references:

- [SUNRISE international series record](https://www.sunrise-inc.co.jp/international/work/detail.php?cid=20) — format, episode count, original air date, staff, and official series synopsis.
- [Official Gundam episode guide](https://en.gundam-official.com/series/mobile-suit-gundam/story/zxx7fu98b3j2d2913b6v3niu) — episode-by-episode chronology and ending facts.

## Causal spine

1. Zeon's mobile suits create an early advantage, but the opening month kills
   about half of humanity and produces stalemate.
2. An unauthorized Zaku attack at Side 7 forces civilian Amuro Ray into the
   Gundam and turns White Base into a refugee ship crewed by survivors.
3. Char's pursuit pushes White Base off course. His betrayal kills Garma;
   Gihren converts private grief into propaganda; Ramba Ral inherits the hunt.
4. Amuro's exhaustion, desertion, and encounter with Ral expose the difference
   between technical talent and maturity. Ryu's sacrifice returns him to a
   crew whose victories keep costing people.
5. Matilda's death and Odessa make White Base strategically important. Kai and
   Miharu make the same war personal from another direction.
6. Reaching Jaburo turns the refugees into an official decoy. Side 6 shows
   Amuro's growing ability, damaged father, and first connection with Lalah.
7. Solomon removes Dozle but costs Sleggar. Texas reveals Char and Sayla's
   Deikun identities. Newtype connection is treated as a weapons program, and
   Lalah dies protecting Char.
8. Degwin's peace attempt is destroyed by Gihren's Solar Ray. Kycilia kills
   Gihren; Char kills Kycilia; Zeon's command structure consumes itself.
9. Gundam and Zeong destroy one another at A Baoa Qu. White Base is lost, but
   Amuro and the crew guide one another out. The weapon is abandoned; the
   relationship survives.

## Edition and terminology traps

- The Federation's **Solar System** mirror weapon at Solomon is not Zeon's
  **Solar Ray** colony laser.
- Episode 15, “Cucuruz Doan's Island,” remains in scope because this is the
  original 43-episode television continuity.
- The game describes Char/Sayla identities only when the television story
  reveals them; it does not backfill later prequel detail.
- Newtypes are summarized as a possibility for perception and connection that
  institutions turn toward targeting. The script avoids presenting later
  franchise metaphysics as settled 1979-series fact.
