## The promise

A strong light novel makes a precise promise about what kind of pleasure the
reader will receive. Record the external hook, emotional question, tone, comic
or dramatic engine, and intended final aftertaste. The premise must still be
interesting after proper nouns and franchise recognition are removed.

Prefer a pressure-bearing premise:

- a person wants something concrete now;
- getting it tests a false belief or vulnerable relationship;
- the setting makes that test visible;
- the likely ending creates an emotional question, not only a plot question.

## Premise slate

Do not commit to the first competent idea. Generate at least five structurally
different candidates; ten is preferred when building a series or story batch.
For each, record the hook, relationship engine, repeatable story engine, ending
pressure, and derivative risk. Select one because it can sustain escalation and
change, not because its title or mascot is immediately cute.

Record research questions that could materially alter the outline. Research is
useful when it introduces constraints, behavior, or texture; it is not a lore
pile added after the story already works.

## The story engine

Define the repeatable source of movement. An engine may be a deadline, ritual,
investigation, rivalry, promise, impossible job, social rule, or accumulating
misunderstanding. Each recurrence must escalate, reveal, or reverse. Repeating
the same joke or observation at a higher word count is not escalation.

Each chapter should change at least one of these:

- what the protagonist knows;
- what the protagonist wants;
- what the relationship permits;
- what failure will cost;
- what the reader believes the story is about.

## Character and relationship

Build characters from behavior under pressure. A useful character record has:

- external want and internal need;
- a false belief that shapes decisions;
- vulnerability and contradiction;
- a behavioral or comic tell;
- a voice system: sentence shape, diction, avoided language, and preferred
  metaphor source;
- a relationship change that can be shown through action.

Do not substitute biography for drama. If two characters can exchange all their
lines without revision, the voice pass has failed.

## Scene causality

Every scene must contain:

1. a present-tense goal;
2. pressure or resistance;
3. a turn that changes the available understanding or options;
4. a decision, refusal, or irreversible reaction;
5. a consequence that causes a later scene;
6. an exit state different from the entrance.

Use `because_of` links to express causality. Coincidence may begin trouble but
should not repeatedly solve it. Quiet scenes still need a turn: permission,
recognition, changed distance, a new question, or a chosen silence can qualify.

## Comedy and tone

Comedy should arise from character logic, physical constraint, social pressure,
misapplied competence, or escalating rules. Track the comic engine and vary the
move: setup, complication, reversal, callback with changed meaning, or sincere
payoff.

Let sincere beats land. A joke immediately after every vulnerable line teaches
the reader not to invest. A callback earns its place when the context changes
what it means.

## Prose and dialogue

Use concrete nouns, active decisions, and selective sensory detail. Vary
sentence rhythm according to point of view and pressure. Make description carry
emotion or information instead of pausing the story for decoration.

Dialogue should create tactics and subtext. Characters may evade, bargain,
perform, protect, tease, or confess; they should not all explain the theme in
finished sentences. Tags and beats should clarify speaker, rhythm, or physical
behavior rather than decorate every line.

## Setup, payoff, and endings

Track each setup by ID, introduction scene, payoff scene, and changed meaning.
A payoff is not merely repeating a phrase. It answers, recontextualizes, or
complicates what was planted.

An ending must resolve the central emotional question through a visible choice
or consequence. Preserve a final image, action, or line with residue. Different
endings need materially different values and costs, not cosmetic labels after a
shared resolution.

## Anti-filler standard

Reject:

- duplicate sentences across scenes;
- long repeated n-grams used as generic connective tissue;
- scene cards whose entering and exit states are equivalent;
- paragraphs that restate the decision without adding pressure or consequence;
- interchangeable sensory lines;
- repeated “quiet beats” that do not change a relationship;
- bulk expansion assembled by rotating a small phrase bank;
- thematic explanations that replace dramatized choices.

The validator reports suspicious repetition, but the editorial pass must judge
intentional refrains. Document a waiver for deliberate repetition and explain
how the meaning changes. Bind the waiver to the exact phrase and cite at least
two affected scenes; never waive an entire repetition class.

## Delight without a quota

Plan signature moments because memorable novels create peaks readers want to
retell. A peak may be a laugh, surprise, tender permission, startling
competence, wonder, recognition, or catharsis. Ask why it belongs only to this
premise and these people. If the answer is “the framework demanded a moment
here,” redesign it.

Use the emotional rhythm map to find long runs with unchanged tension, warmth,
humor, and wonder. The map is diagnostic. A deliberately still run may be
artistically correct, but it needs specific editorial justification; do not
replace it with mechanically alternating moods.

Genre polish means delivering the pleasure a reader came for while making the
delivery specific. The freshness move changes how an expectation is dramatized,
not merely the names of familiar objects.

## Presentation and series polish

An edition is part of the reading experience. Cover promise, typography, scene
breaks, image placement, alt text, front/back matter, and closing-page rhythm
must support the prose's tone. Illustrations need narrative purpose, consistent
design, ImageGen provenance, and human approval.

For a series, protect both satisfaction and appetite. Resolve the volume promise
completely while moving the longer arc, preserving canon, and leaving a future
hook more meaningful than an arbitrary cliffhanger.

## Quality scorecard

Score every category from 1–5 and cite at least two scene-specific pieces of
evidence. Four is the release floor; five should be rare.

1. Premise and reader promise
2. Causal structure and escalation
3. Character specificity and change
4. Relationship dynamics
5. Scene turns and consequences
6. Dialogue and voice separation
7. Prose precision and rhythm
8. Comedy or tonal control
9. Setup, payoff, and continuity
10. Ending impact and aftertaste
11. Signature delight and memorability
12. Chapter momentum and reader pull
13. Genre satisfaction and freshness
14. Presentation and publication polish
15. Series continuity or standalone completeness

Never average categories. Any score below four blocks release. Record a
remaining risk even for a five.
