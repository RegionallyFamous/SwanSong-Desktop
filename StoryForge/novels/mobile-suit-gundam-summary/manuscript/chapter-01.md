# Last Tour Before Closing

A preferred-route source manuscript for the branching visual novel.

<!-- scene: scene-01 -->

## A brilliant Gundam display is dimmed until the ruined Side 7 lights remain visible

<!-- voice: protagonist -->

**Docent RX:** Welcome to the Universal Century History Museum. We close in twenty minutes, and tonight's final visitors are already waiting at the first gallery. Docent Zaku arrives with a cart of casualty records taller than either of us. The lobby clock advances one minute out of professional concern. Our promise is simple: follow each decision, preserve each consequence, and leave no one reduced to a colored light.

<!-- voice: foil -->

**Docent Zaku:** Before we begin: this hall once called itself a victory chronicle. We revised it. Every display now follows people, decisions, and consequences instead of polishing one faction's trophies. The archive doors seal at closing, and RX has assigned me a footnote cart with one bad wheel. I punch our first stop: Universal Century 0079.

<!-- voice: foil -->

**Docent Zaku:** Side 3 declares itself the Principality of Zeon and fights the Earth Federation for independence. Mobile suits let Zeon overcome a huge numerical disadvantage. During the opening month, the war kills roughly half of humanity before settling into a terrible stalemate. The map has two faction colors and far too many empty lights. Neither color gets to claim the dead as a victory statistic.

<!-- voice: protagonist -->

**Docent RX:** At Side 7, the Federation secretly tests Operation V: Gundam, Guncannon, Guntank, and the new ship White Base. Char Aznable sends three Zakus to investigate. A Zeon pilot attacks without orders, turning reconnaissance into a colony disaster. Fifteen-year-old Amuro Ray finds the Gundam manual after his neighbors are killed. He climbs into his father's machine because every trained answer around him is already gone.

<!-- voice: protagonist -->

**Docent RX:** Amuro activates the RX-78-2 and defeats two Zakus through technical instinct, not heroic readiness. That success saves survivors and traps him in a role nobody asked whether he could bear. The museum's Gundam display lights brilliantly. Zaku turns down the victory lamp until the ruined colony can still be seen behind it. A weapon has risen, but the story has begun with refugees.

<!-- voice: protagonist -->

**Docent RX:** I punch SIDE 7 into the tour ticket. The paper strip now has one bright stop and an alarming amount of blank space. Zaku unfolds the rest across the rail: Luna II, Earth, Jaburo, Side 6, Solomon, and A Baoa Qu. This is not a scoreboard. The ticket remembers decisions, not points, and it never awards a winner. Each punch means someone made a choice that narrowed the next set of choices. The ribbon also keeps causes from vanishing behind famous machines: a shortage can shape a duel, a funeral can redirect an army, and one frightened return can matter more than a captured base. If the chain ever feels crowded, the visitor guide can reopen the people or command plaque at the nearest gallery.

<!-- scene: scene-02 -->

## A route display marks atmospheric reentry as an unfortunate detour lasting most of a campaign

<!-- voice: foil -->

**Docent Zaku:** White Base escapes carrying civilians, wounded soldiers, and three prototype mobile suits. Junior officer Bright Noa inherits command because the adults above him are dead or injured. Fraw Bow cares for three orphaned children while civilians become pilots, gunners, and mechanics. Char pursues them because Gundam could change the war. The ship's crew is whoever survived and kept moving.

<!-- voice: protagonist -->

**Docent RX:** White Base attacks Char's supply ship because running without supplies is another kind of surrender. At Luna II, Federation officers arrest the refugees and confiscate their equipment for breaking protocol. Their own side treats survival as suspicious paperwork. Char's attack gives the crew a chance to escape. The official rescue system has now been outperformed by the emergency exit twice.

<!-- voice: foil -->

**Docent Zaku:** Amuro launches the Core Fighter to seek Federation contact, proving how isolated the ship remains. Refugees later demand to leave, and a temporary ceasefire reveals civilians trying to survive between armies. By the time Garma attacks again, Amuro is too exhausted to accept another launch order. The crew does not become dependable by feeling brave. They become dependable by naming fear, failing one another, and returning to their stations anyway.

<!-- voice: foil -->

**Docent Zaku:** During atmospheric reentry, Char attacks where one mistake means burning up. Amuro must learn both combat and physics under fire. Char's pressure knocks White Base off course into Zeon-controlled North America. The route display marks this as an unfortunate detour. RX adds that the detour lasts most of the Earth campaign. I replace unfortunate with structurally decisive.

<!-- voice: protagonist -->

**Docent RX:** This junction once split the collection into Personnel and Strategy halls. The White Base gallery keeps us beside the crew as strangers become a family. The war-map gallery follows the ambitions that steer armies. Both halls meet again at the final evacuation display because they preserve different evidence from the same war. History does not change with the doorway. What changes is what the room lets us notice.

<!-- voice: protagonist -->

**Docent RX:** Garma Zabi hunts White Base across North America while Amuro sometimes refuses to launch from exhaustion. Refugees attempt to leave the ship, then discover that the battlefield follows them. The crew votes, argues, improvises, and keeps one another alive. Bright and Amuro are both teenagers forced into authority by adults who are absent or demanding. Their conflict is not discipline versus cowardice; it is two frightened people using the only controls they have.

<!-- voice: foil -->

**Docent Zaku:** Char pretends to guide his friend Garma, then feeds him false tactical information. Garma charges into White Base's ambush and dies. His lover Icelina launches a doomed revenge attack. Gihren Zabi turns Garma's funeral into propaganda, while veteran Ramba Ral receives the next pursuit order. Private grief becomes another weapon before anyone has time to understand it.

<!-- voice: foil -->

**Docent Zaku:** The desert case begins with two commanders who do not receive what they need. Bright has a crew but almost no experience; Ramba Ral has experience but loses supplies to rival Zeon politics. Amuro sees only the veteran who can defeat him. The display shows the larger trap: frightened young command on one side, proud under-supported command on the other, and civilians between them. Hamon and Ral still choose loyalty to each other and their soldiers, which makes the exhibit warmer and more terrible. Competence cannot repair a command structure that withholds support, and youth cannot be hurried into trust by issuing another order. Ral's strength will expose Amuro's immaturity. Ral's own system will help kill him.

<!-- scene: scene-03 -->

## The memorial light remains dark for one full breath before the Odessa map resumes

<!-- voice: protagonist -->

**Docent RX:** Amuro meets his mother and discovers that survival has made him someone she can barely recognize. Ordinary Zeon soldiers later attach bombs to Gundam, and Amuro removes them by hand under a deadline. On Cucuruz Doan's island, a Zeon deserter protects children and refuses the war's machinery altogether. Three exhibits break the faction labels in different ways: family can fail to understand, enemies can be desperate, and a soldier can choose to stop.

<!-- voice: foil -->

**Docent Zaku:** Sayla Mass takes Gundam without permission because she needs to learn whether Char is her lost brother. Amuro, exhausted and treated like replaceable equipment, quarrels with Bright and deserts with Gundam. He attacks a Zeon mine alone, believing one gifted pilot can solve a war. The mine is not decisive. The desert is very large. Even the museum map refuses to fit his plan on one card.

<!-- voice: foil -->

**Docent Zaku:** Ramba Ral's experience exposes Amuro's immaturity, yet Ral is denied the support his mission needs. He boards White Base, recognizes Sayla as Artesia, and dies rather than surrender. Hamon attacks again in grief. Ryu Jose sacrifices himself to save Amuro and the ship. Courage does not protect the crew from loss. The memorial light stays dark for one full breath before our tour continues.

<!-- voice: protagonist -->

**Docent RX:** Bright collapses under command strain while M'Quve closes a trap around White Base. Lieutenant Matilda keeps the ship supplied, then dies during the Black Tri-Stars' attack. At Odessa, Amuro exposes Federation traitor Elran and stops M'Quve's nuclear strike. The Federation wins its major Earth campaign and breaks Zeon's foothold. White Base has changed the war, but Amuro experiences victory as another name added to glass.

<!-- voice: protagonist -->

**Docent RX:** The Odessa map tries to illuminate every successful arrow at once. I cover the power switch with the ticket. Ryu's name sits beside the place where he bought the crew time; Matilda's sits beside the supplies that let them arrive at all. Around them, mechanics, medics, and pilots kept the ship moving through failures no campaign arrow can display. Stopping a nuclear strike matters. Breaking Zeon's Earth foothold matters. Neither result makes grief an accounting error. A useful record must hold the outcome, the labor, and the price in the same frame. We punch ODESSA only after the memorial names are readable.

<!-- scene: scene-04 -->

## An empty chair and two lunch tins replace a red token on the spy exhibit

<!-- voice: foil -->

**Docent Zaku:** Char returns under Kycilia and attacks Belfast with amphibious mobile suits. Kai Shiden deserts, meets Miharu, and learns she spies for Zeon to support younger siblings. She boards White Base, then chooses to help Kai defend it when the attack becomes real. Miharu dies during the battle. Kai returns to the crew still skeptical of institutions, but no longer pretending that detachment keeps people safe.

<!-- voice: protagonist -->

**Docent RX:** The spy exhibit once displayed Miharu as a red token crossing a blue ship. Zaku replaces it with an empty chair and two small lunch tins for the siblings she supported. Kai's return matters because nobody orders it. He chooses the flawed crew he knows over the safety of pretending not to care. White Base is becoming a home precisely because its members can leave, understand the cost, and still come back.

<!-- voice: protagonist -->

**Docent RX:** The personnel plaque is arranged by what each person keeps moving. Bright gives frightened orders and slowly learns to listen. Mirai reads the ship and its people before the instruments finish arguing. Sayla carries a family history she refuses to let Char choose for her. Kai doubts every institution, yet returns when people need him. Amuro begins as the best machine operator in the room and must become someone who can live among the crew. The guide closes and returns us to Jaburo.

<!-- voice: protagonist -->

**Docent RX:** White Base finally reaches Jaburo and accidentally leads Char to Federation headquarters. Woody dies opposing Char. Katz, Letz, and Kikka discover saboteurs and help save the new mass-produced GMs. Gundam's combat data is now reshaping the whole army. The refugees are formally absorbed into the military and sent back to space as a conspicuous decoy. Reaching safety has only made them more useful.

<!-- voice: foil -->

**Docent Zaku:** Char and other Zeon forces try to stop White Base before it joins the main fleet. The crew's accumulated skill now produces victories that once seemed impossible. At neutral Side 6, Amuro meets his father Tem Ray, mentally damaged since Side 7 and fixated on an obsolete machine part. Amuro has outgrown both his father and the hope that better machinery alone can end war. Neutral ground offers recognition, not escape.

<!-- voice: protagonist -->

**Docent RX:** Side 6 also shows lives that continued outside White Base: Mirai faces Cameron, and the crew briefly sees what ordinary safety might have looked like. Then Conscon attacks outside the neutral colony and Amuro destroys his force with alarming speed. The museum counter cannot decide whether to flash SKILL or DANGER, so it flashes both. Amuro's ability is growing faster than the adults around him can understand its human cost.

<!-- voice: foil -->

**Docent Zaku:** Side 6 gives Amuro three reflections instead of safety. His father sees only a machine part; Cameron offers Mirai the life she might have chosen; Lalah recognizes Amuro before either can explain how. One reflection is damaged memory, one is an unlived future, and one is a new kind of connection. The war immediately asks which of them can be made useful. We punch SIDE 6, and the ticket mechanism hesitates over whether recognition counts as progress or warning.

<!-- scene: scene-05 -->

## Two gallery-light circles almost overlap, then pull apart after Lalah's death

<!-- voice: protagonist -->

**Docent RX:** At Side 6, Amuro meets Lalah Sune before he understands her bond with Char. They recognize something in one another immediately. Amuro's growing awareness also lets him destroy conventional attackers with frightening speed. Newtypes may represent wider perception, empathy, and connection. Both militaries mostly see faster targeting. The next gallery asks whether we follow the people who might understand one another, or the powers trying to own that possibility.

<!-- voice: foil -->

**Docent Zaku:** Before the final archive divides, Solomon fills the glass. The Federation uses Solar System to burn through the fortress defenses; Dozle answers with Big Zam. Sleggar strikes the mobile armor knowing he may not return, and Amuro finishes the opening he creates. Dozle dies fighting for a regime that has already spent his family as symbols. The fortress model is enormous, but the missing chair beneath it controls the room. That contrast is the point: strategy changes history at one scale while absence changes every ordinary ritual at another. The map calls the fortress captured. The roster calls one station empty. Both records are true, so the exhibit keeps both lights on.

<!-- voice: foil -->

**Docent Zaku:** The command plaque has too many arrows and one family at the center. Degwin founded the Principality but can no longer control what his children made of it. Gihren turns ideology and family deaths into fuel. Kycilia builds rival command networks and recruits Char without understanding his revenge. Dozle fights for family and soldiers while serving the same state. Char is Casval Deikun inside their machine, waiting for openings. The plaque folds shut before its arrows can stab another label.

<!-- voice: protagonist -->

**Docent RX:** The Federation assaults Solomon. Sleggar Law sacrifices himself to open a path against Dozle Zabi's Big Zam, and Amuro finishes the fight. Dozle dies defending the fortress, another Zabi branch cut away by the war it commands. The victory lamp ignites, notices the empty crew station, and dims itself. White Base continues because grief and duty have become inseparable.

<!-- voice: foil -->

**Docent Zaku:** At Texas Colony, Amuro defeats M'Quve while Char and Sayla confront their hidden family history. Char is Casval Rem Deikun; Sayla is Artesia. They are children of Zeon Zum Deikun, and Char entered the Zabi state to avenge their family. Sayla refuses to make revenge her home. Char's divided loyalties now pull against Amuro, Lalah, Zeon, and the mission that created his mask.

<!-- voice: protagonist -->

**Docent RX:** Zeon deploys Challia Bull and then Lalah as Newtype weapons. Amuro and Lalah communicate across the battlefield with a depth neither side's command understands. She remains loyal to Char and shields him from Amuro's attack. Amuro kills her. Mutual recognition arrives inside machinery built to prevent it. Char and Amuro turn their shared grief into blame, and the gallery lights become two circles that almost overlap.

<!-- voice: foil -->

**Docent Zaku:** The two circles remain on the wall after the machines vanish. One holds Amuro's grief, one Char's, and Lalah's light crosses both. Neither pilot can accept that understanding arrived too late to prevent the shot. So each turns pain into a simpler story about the other. The exhibit refuses that simplification. It leaves the circles unfinished, because a connection can be real even when the people inside it fail.

<!-- scene: scene-06 -->

## The broken exit arrow becomes a warm path while the tour ticket receives its HOME punch

<!-- voice: foil -->

**Docent Zaku:** Degwin Zabi secretly approaches Federation commander Revil to seek peace. Gihren fires the Solar Ray colony laser, killing both men and wrecking much of the Federation fleet. Kycilia confirms that Gihren knowingly killed their father, then kills Gihren and takes command. The Zabi family board finally pulls itself off the wall. A dictatorship that weaponized everyone has reached its last available target: itself.

<!-- voice: protagonist -->

**Docent RX:** The surviving Federation force attacks A Baoa Qu. Char sorties in the unfinished Zeong and duels Amuro until Zeong and Gundam destroy one another. They continue the fight on foot, as if losing the machines might finally reveal what the feud is for. Sayla interrupts them. Char remembers the Zabis were his original target and kills Kycilia as she tries to escape. The regime collapses around personal revenge.

<!-- voice: foil -->

**Docent Zaku:** White Base is destroyed inside the fortress, but its people are still alive. Amuro reaches outward and guides them through smoke, wreckage, and closing paths. Their voices guide him back in return. He escapes in the Core Fighter and rejoins the crew. The Gundam is left behind. The boy who began alone inside a weapon ends by finding the people he wants to return to.

<!-- voice: protagonist -->

**Docent RX:** The war ends soon afterward, but our tour does not award a clean heroic badge. I remove HERO from my docent hook. Zaku removes VILLAIN from his. We replace both with WITNESS. The broken exit path finally points toward the open door, and our ticket receives its last punch: HOME. The museum clock reaches zero, then waits for everyone to leave together.

<!-- voice: foil -->

**Museum Lights:** The ticket glows HOME. Two small docents hold the door while every reflected name crosses into morning.
